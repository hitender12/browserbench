# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in BrowserBench a harassment-free
experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or
invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community — and to keep the
benchmark itself honest. BrowserBench only works if people trust its
results, so integrity is a community norm here, not just a technical
rule.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experience
  levels; giving and gracefully accepting constructive criticism.
- Focusing on what is best for the community and for the trustworthiness
  of the benchmark, not for one agent, company, or result.
- Showing empathy towards other community members, including people whose
  first language is not English.
- Reporting benchmark problems honestly: a flaky task, a gameable success
  condition, or a misleading result is a defect to report, not an
  advantage to keep quiet about.
- Reviewing pull requests on their merits, explaining design decisions,
  and assuming good faith unless there is evidence to the contrary.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form, including
  sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or
  advances.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Publishing others' private information (such as a physical or email
  address) without explicit permission.
- **Gaming the benchmark**: designing or promoting a task, adapter, or
  scoring change that favours a particular agent, framework, or model;
  peeking at hidden evaluation state; misrepresenting results (for
  example, reporting a score without its task versions, seed, agent
  version, and configuration); or claiming a result that cannot be
  reproduced from its published artifact.
- Submitting results, tasks, or adapters that test against real users'
  accounts or real websites without permission, or that use non-synthetic
  personal data.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — the repository,
issues, pull requests, discussions, and any project chat or events — and
also applies when an individual is officially representing the project in
public spaces (for example, presenting benchmark results at a conference
or in a blog post). Representation includes using an official project
email address or social media account, or acting as an appointed
representative at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our
standards of acceptable behaviour and will take appropriate and fair
corrective action in response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive, or harmful. This includes benchmark-integrity
violations: a task or result found to be gamed may be removed, reverted,
or relabelled, and the contributor may be asked to correct the record.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject
comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions
that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct, and to communicate
reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, or you discover
benchmark gaming or misrepresented results, report it to the project
maintainers privately — do not post the details publicly. Use GitHub's
private reporting on the repository (or, for security-sensitive matters,
the private vulnerability reporting flow described in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)) and include what happened, where, and who
was involved. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly
and fairly, and the reporter will be kept informed of the outcome where
possible.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security
of the reporter of any incident.

## Enforcement guidelines

Community impact and the response follow the Contributor Covenant
enforcement ladder:

1. **Correction** — A private, written warning about inappropriate
   behaviour (or a request to correct a misleading result or task), with
   an explanation of why it is a problem.
2. **Warning** — A warning with consequences for continued behaviour,
   such as a temporary ban from interacting with the project spaces.
3. **Temporary ban** — A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or
   public communication with the community for a specified period of time.
4. **Permanent ban** — A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction
   within the community.

For benchmark-integrity violations, the corrective action may also
include invalidating the affected results and publishing a correction in
the changelog or methodology docs, so the public record stays accurate.
